**Within-budget strings.** `"hello".truncate_bytes(5)` and `(10)` both print `"hello"`, `bytesize=5`, `equal?=false`. `"👍👍".truncate_bytes(8)` and `(16)` print the original 8-byte string. `"".truncate_bytes(0)` prints `""`, also a different object.

That proves the `bytesize <= truncate_to` path returns a copy of the whole receiver and never truncates or shares identity. If a refactor returns `self`, `equal?` flips true.

**ASCII truncation.** `"abcdefghij"` (10 bytes) with default `"…"` (3 bytes):

- 9 → `"abcdef…"` (9)
- 5 → `"ab…"` (5)
- 3 → `"…"` (3)
- 2, 1, 0 → `ArgumentError` (`Omission "…" is 3, larger than …`)

That proves the method cuts on whole characters, accounts for omission bytes in the budget, and may return only the omission when no prefix fits. `bytesize <= n` on every non-error row is the global cap.

**Multibyte truncation.** `"👍👍👍👍"` is 16 bytes (4×4). Default omission:

- 16 → original
- 15 → `"👍👍👍…"` (15)
- 12 and 11 → `"👍👍…"` (11)
- 8 and 7 → `"👍…"` (7)
- 5, 4, 3 → `"…"` (3)

15 vs 16 proves a 4-byte codepoint is not split to steal one byte. 12 vs 11 printing the same 11-byte string proves slack is left unused rather than emitting a partial 👍. If a refactor used `byteslice`, 15 would become an invalid UTF-8 string.

**Grapheme clusters.** `"a ❤️ b"` is clusters `["a"," ","❤️"," ","b"]` with sizes `[1,1,6,1,1]`. With `omission: nil`:

- 0 → `""`
- 1 → `"a"`
- 2..7 → `"a "` (2 bytes)
- 8 → `"a ❤️"`
- 9 → `"a ❤️ "`
- 10 → original

`"👩‍❤️‍👩"` is one 20-byte cluster; `truncate_bytes(13, omission: nil)` → `""`. `"a 👩‍❤️‍👩"` at 13 → `"a "`.

That proves the cut is grapheme-cluster-safe, not codepoint-safe: ❤️ is kept or dropped as a unit, and a ZWJ sequence that does not fit is dropped entirely. Codepoint truncation would emit a broken heart or a lone 👩 at 13.

**Exact byte boundaries.** `"café"` is `[1,1,1,2]`. With `omission: nil`: budgets 1..3 take `"c"` / `"ca"` / `"caf"`; 4 stays `"caf"` (3 bytes); 5 returns the original. `"e\u0301"` is one 3-byte cluster: budgets 1 and 2 → `""`; 3 → the cluster.

That proves a cut that lands inside a codepoint or inside a combining-mark cluster does not emit a prefix of that cluster. `"caf"` at 4 is the witness that the extra byte is refused.

**Custom and nil omissions.** Same 16-byte 👍 string:

| call | result | bytes |
|---|---|---|
| `omission: nil`, 15 | `"👍👍👍"` | 12 |
| `omission: " "` , 15 | `"👍👍👍 "` | 13 |
| `omission: "🖖"`, 15 | `"👍👍🖖"` | 12 |
| `omission: "..."`, 15 | `"👍👍👍..."` | 15 |
| `omission: ""`, 15 | `"👍👍👍"` | 12 |
| `omission: nil`, 5 | `"👍"` | 4 |
| `omission: " "`, 5 | `"👍 "` | 5 |
| `omission: "🖖"`, 5 | `"🖖"` | 4 |
| `omission: nil`, 4 | `"👍"` | 4 |
| `omission: " "`, 4 | `" "` | 1 |
| `omission: nil`, 0 | `""` | 0 |

Nil matching `""` proves `omission ||= ""`. Different omissions at the same budget prove the reserved tail is `omission.bytesize`, not a character count. `" "` at 4 vs `"👍"` at 4 with nil proves a 1-byte omission can leave no room for a 4-byte prefix.

**Omission equal to or larger than the budget.** `"…"` is 3 bytes, `"🖖"` is 4, `"..."` is 3.

- `truncate_bytes(3)` → `"…"` (a dup; `equal?` with the default omission string is false)
- `truncate_bytes(2)` → `ArgumentError`
- `truncate_bytes(4, omission: "🖖")` → `"🖖"`
- `truncate_bytes(3, omission: "🖖")` → `ArgumentError` (`Omission "🖖" is 4, larger than the truncation length of 3 bytes`)
- `truncate_bytes(3, omission: "...")` → `"..."`
- `truncate_bytes(0, omission: nil)` / `""` → `""`
- `truncate_bytes(0, omission: " ")` → `ArgumentError`

Equal-size proves the omission-only branch, with no prefix. Larger proves the raise, including the exact message. A silent clip of the omission would fail these rows.

**Encoding and subclass preservation.** Under budget, encoding is copied: UTF-8 stays UTF-8; `"abcdefghij"` tagged US-ASCII stays US-ASCII; ASCII-8BIT stays ASCII-8BIT; `"hello".encode(UTF-16LE).truncate_bytes(100)` stays UTF-16LE.

On truncation with default `"…"`, US-ASCII and ASCII-8BIT both become UTF-8 (`"ab…"`). UTF-16LE at 6 raises `Encoding::CompatibilityError`. That is current behavior: the UTF-8 omission is appended as-is. A refactor that transcodes or that preserves ASCII-8BIT here is a change.

`class MyString < String`: `MyString.new("abcdefghij").truncate_bytes(5)` and `(20)` are both `MyString`. `MyString.new("👍👍👍👍").truncate_bytes(4, omission: "🖖")` is `String`, not `MyString`. That proves `dup` / `self.class.new` on the receiver paths, and `omission.dup` on the equal-budget path.

**Receiver mutation.** After every call above, the receiver `inspect` is unchanged. `result.equal?(receiver)` is always false. Appending `"X"` / `"Y"` / `"Z"` to the result leaves the receiver without those characters, including the under-budget dup and the `omission.dup` result.

That proves every return is a new unfrozen string and the method is non-destructive. Sharing the receiver or the omission object would make those appends show up on the original.
